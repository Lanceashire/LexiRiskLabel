\begin{abstract}
Lending decisions determine which repayment outcomes enter future training data. When feedback from a rejected region requires approving applicants, an information-oriented selector must justify both the observations it forgoes and the predicted loss it incurs. We study this trade-off with lexicographic risk-budgeted feedback acquisition (LRBE), which computes the maximum feasible exploratory count before optimizing a secondary score. Random-order replay covers four credit datasets and five budget ratios, and a five-policy experiment compares FAVE with bootstrap-committee information gain (IG). At $\rho=0.1$, LRBE-IG acquires 31.3, 49.5, and 40.1 more labels than QualityFirst-IG on HMEQ, LendingClub, and UCI, respectively. Recall estimates span improvement and deterioration, and UCI Brier error increases by 0.000156 [0.000019, 0.000298]. Relative to minimum-risk selection, LRBE-IG uses 28,765.5 more predicted-risk units on HMEQ and 27,025.7 more on LendingClub, again with mixed Recall and Brier estimates. FAVE and randomized controls exhibit the same separation between preserving count and choosing composition. The results support evaluating acquisition by feedback quantity, predicted-risk use, composition, and downstream performance together.
\keywords{Selective labels \and Feedback acquisition \and Risk budgets \and Credit prediction}
\end{abstract}
