\begin{abstract}
Lending decisions determine which repayment outcomes become available for future learning. Under a predicted-loss budget, selecting applicants for information value may improve feedback composition while reducing observable outcomes. We study this trade-off with lexicographic risk-budgeted feedback acquisition (LRBE), which computes the maximum feasible exploratory count before optimizing a secondary score. Random-order replay covers four credit datasets and five budget ratios. A five-policy experiment replaces FAVE's linearized score with bootstrap-committee information gain (IG). At $\rho=0.1$, LRBE-IG acquires 31.3 more labels than QualityFirst-IG on HMEQ, 49.5 on LendingClub, and 40.1 on UCI, but the corresponding Recall intervals include zero; its UCI Brier score is slightly worse. Relative to minimum-risk selection, LRBE-IG uses 28,765.5 more predicted-risk units on HMEQ and 27,025.7 more on LendingClub without a Recall or Brier interval excluding zero. FAVE and randomized controls show a similar distinction between preserving count and choosing composition. Minimum-risk maximum-count selection therefore provides a practical reference: an acquisition score should be evaluated by the feedback it displaces, the predicted risk it consumes, and its downstream predictive return.
\keywords{Selective labels \and Feedback acquisition \and Risk budgets \and Credit prediction}
\end{abstract}
